# Slope and Rate of Change

## Overview

The **slope** of a line measures its steepness and direction — how much the $y$-value changes for every one-unit increase in $x$. A positive slope means the line rises left to right; negative means it falls; zero means horizontal; undefined means vertical. Slope also has a real-world interpretation as a rate of change.

## Key Idea

Given any two distinct points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ on a line:

$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$$

The fraction is rise (vertical change) divided by run (horizontal change). The order you subtract must be consistent: if you use point 2 minus point 1 in the numerator, you must also use point 2 minus point 1 in the denominator.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1: Find the slope through $(1, 3)$ and $(4, 9)$**

Apply the formula with $(x_1, y_1) = (1, 3)$ and $(x_2, y_2) = (4, 9)$:

$$m = \frac{9 - 3}{4 - 1} = \frac{6}{3} = 2$$

This means for every 1 unit increase in $x$, $y$ increases by 2. The line rises steadily from left to right.

---

**Example 2: Find the slope through $(-2, 5)$ and $(3, 5)$**

Both $y$-values are the same, so:

$$m = \frac{5 - 5}{3 - (-2)} = \frac{0}{5} = 0$$

A slope of zero means no vertical change at all — the line is perfectly horizontal. Any horizontal line $y = c$ has slope 0.

---

**Example 3: A car travels 210 km in 3 hours. What is the rate of change of distance with respect to time?**

Rate of change is slope: $m = \frac{\Delta d}{\Delta t} = \frac{210 \text{ km}}{3 \text{ h}} = 70 \text{ km/h}$. This is the car's speed — slope is the rate at which distance accumulates per hour. On a distance-time graph, the slope of the line is the speed.

## Common Mistakes

- **Subtracting coordinates in mismatched order.** Using $(y_2 - y_1)$ on top but $(x_1 - x_2)$ on bottom flips the sign of the result. Choose one order and be consistent throughout.
- **Confusing zero slope with undefined slope.** A horizontal line ($y = 5$) has slope 0 — the numerator is zero. A vertical line ($x = 3$) has undefined slope — the denominator is zero. Dividing by zero is undefined, not zero.
- **Mixing up which coordinate is $x$ and which is $y$.** Always write your points as $(x, y)$ and label them before substituting.
