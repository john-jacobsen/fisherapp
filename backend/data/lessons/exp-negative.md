# Negative Exponents

## Overview

A **negative exponent** does not make a number negative — it signals a reciprocal. $a^{-n}$ means "one divided by $a^n$." Understanding why this is true (not just that it is true) makes every problem with negative exponents straightforward.

## Key Idea

$$a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n} \quad (a \ne 0)$$

The reason comes from a pattern. Start with $a^3 = a \cdot a \cdot a$ and divide by $a$ repeatedly:

$$a^3 \to a^2 \to a^1 \to a^0 = 1 \to a^{-1} = \frac{1}{a} \to a^{-2} = \frac{1}{a^2}$$

Each step divides the previous result by $a$. The exponent decreases by 1 every time. So negative exponents are not a strange special case — they are the same pattern, continued past zero.

Moving a factor across the fraction bar flips the sign of its exponent: $\frac{x^{-2}}{1} = \frac{1}{x^2}$ and $\frac{1}{y^{-3}} = y^3$.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1: Evaluate $5^{-2}$**

The negative exponent means you take the reciprocal of $5^2$, not a negative number. $5^2 = 25$, so you divide 1 by that result.

$$5^{-2} = \frac{1}{5^2} = \frac{1}{25}$$

The answer is a small positive fraction, not anything negative.

---

**Example 2: Simplify $x^{-3} \cdot x^5$**

You have two powers of the same base, so the product rule applies: add the exponents. $-3 + 5 = 2$, giving a positive exponent in the final answer. No reciprocal is needed once you combine them.

$$x^{-3} \cdot x^5 = x^{-3+5} = x^2$$

---

**Example 3: Simplify $\dfrac{4x^{-2}}{y^{-3}}$**

Each factor with a negative exponent moves across the fraction bar, and its exponent becomes positive. $x^{-2}$ is in the numerator, so it moves to the denominator. $y^{-3}$ is in the denominator, so it moves to the numerator. The coefficient 4 has no exponent issue — it stays put.

$$\frac{4x^{-2}}{y^{-3}} = \frac{4y^3}{x^2}$$

## Common Mistakes

- **Thinking $a^{-n}$ produces a negative result.** The output is always positive (assuming $a > 0$): $2^{-3} = \frac{1}{8}$, not $-8$.
- **Applying the reciprocal to the whole expression instead of just the factor with the negative exponent.** In $4x^{-2}$, only $x^{-2}$ flips — the 4 does not. The result is $\frac{4}{x^2}$, not $\frac{1}{4x^2}$.
- **Confusing $(-a)^n$ with $a^{-n}$.** A negative base and a negative exponent are two completely different things. $(-2)^3 = -8$, but $2^{-3} = \frac{1}{8}$.

## Quick Check

1. Evaluate $4^{-2}$
2. Simplify $\dfrac{1}{b^{-5}}$
3. Simplify $\dfrac{3a^{-1}}{b^{-2}}$

*(Answers: $\dfrac{1}{16}$, $b^5$, $\dfrac{3b^2}{a}$)*
