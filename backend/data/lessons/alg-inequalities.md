# Linear Inequalities

## Overview

A **linear inequality** looks like a linear equation but uses $<$, $>$, $\le$, or $\ge$ instead of $=$. The solution is a range of values — an interval — rather than a single number. You solve it with the same algebraic steps as an equation, with one crucial exception: the inequality sign flips when you multiply or divide by a negative number.

## Key Idea

Solve like an equation, but reverse the inequality when multiplying or dividing by a negative:

$$\text{If } a > b \text{ and } c < 0, \text{ then } ac < bc$$

This happens because multiplying by a negative number reflects values across zero on the number line, reversing their order. Write the solution in interval notation or as a compound inequality.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1: Solve $3x - 5 > 7$**

Add 5 to both sides (no sign change since we're adding, not multiplying by negative):

$$3x > 12$$

Divide by 3 (positive, so no sign flip):

$$x > 4$$

The solution is all $x$ greater than 4, written as $(4, \infty)$. The boundary $x = 4$ is excluded because the inequality is strict ($>$, not $\ge$).

---

**Example 2: Solve $-2x + 6 \ge 10$**

Subtract 6 from both sides: $-2x \ge 4$.

Divide by $-2$. Since $-2 < 0$, the inequality flips from $\ge$ to $\le$:

$$x \le -2$$

Solution: $(-\infty, -2]$. The bracket indicates $-2$ is included because the original inequality was $\ge$.

---

**Example 3: Solve the compound inequality $1 < 2x + 3 \le 9$**

Perform the same operations on all three parts simultaneously. Subtract 3 throughout:

$$1 - 3 < 2x \le 9 - 3 \implies -2 < 2x \le 6$$

Divide all parts by 2 (positive, no flip):

$$-1 < x \le 3$$

Solution: $(-1, 3]$. The left boundary is excluded (strict $<$) and the right is included ($\le$).

## Common Mistakes

- **Forgetting to flip the inequality when dividing by a negative.** This is the most common error. When you divide by $-2$, the direction reverses: $\ge$ becomes $\le$. Always check the sign of your divisor.
- **Using a solid boundary line for strict inequalities when graphing.** Strict inequalities ($<$, $>$) use an open circle on a number line or a dashed line on a coordinate plane. Non-strict ($\le$, $\ge$) use a closed circle or solid line.
- **Treating compound inequalities as two separate problems and losing track of direction.** In $a < x \le b$, $a$ must be less than $b$. If your result has $3 < x \le -1$, something went wrong.
