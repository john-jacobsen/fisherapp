# Two-Step Equations

## Overview

A **two-step equation** requires two inverse operations to isolate the variable. The key is applying those operations in the right order. You undo the operations in reverse — the last thing that was done to $x$ gets undone first.

## Key Idea

For an equation of the form $ax + b = c$, think about what happened to $x$: it was first multiplied by $a$, then $b$ was added. To reverse that process, undo the addition first, then the multiplication.

$$ax + b = c \implies ax = c - b \implies x = \frac{c - b}{a}$$

Why this order? If you divided by $a$ first, you would get $x + \frac{b}{a} = \frac{c}{a}$, which introduces fractions unnecessarily. Subtracting first keeps the numbers cleaner and avoids that complication.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1: Solve $3x + 4 = 19$**

The expression $3x + 4$ adds 4 to the quantity $3x$. Undo the addition first by subtracting 4 from both sides. That leaves $3x$ isolated on the left. Then undo the multiplication by dividing both sides by 3.

$$3x + 4 - 4 = 19 - 4 \implies 3x = 15 \implies x = \frac{15}{3} = 5$$

Check: $3(5) + 4 = 15 + 4 = 19$. Correct.

---

**Example 2: Solve $2x - 7 = 11$**

Here, 7 is subtracted from $2x$. The inverse of subtracting 7 is adding 7, so add 7 to both sides first. That clears the constant term and leaves $2x = 18$. Dividing both sides by 2 finishes the job.

$$2x - 7 + 7 = 11 + 7 \implies 2x = 18 \implies x = \frac{18}{2} = 9$$

Check: $2(9) - 7 = 18 - 7 = 11$. Correct.

---

**Example 3: Solve $\dfrac{x}{5} + 3 = 8$**

The variable $x$ is divided by 5, then 3 is added. Reverse that: subtract 3 first, then multiply by 5. Subtracting 3 from both sides gives $\frac{x}{5} = 5$. Multiplying both sides by 5 clears the division and leaves $x = 25$.

$$\frac{x}{5} + 3 - 3 = 8 - 3 \implies \frac{x}{5} = 5 \implies x = 5 \cdot 5 = 25$$

Check: $\frac{25}{5} + 3 = 5 + 3 = 8$. Correct.

## Common Mistakes

- **Dividing before subtracting when the equation is $ax + b = c$.** This is not wrong — it is just messier. Dividing first turns $b$ into a fraction, making the second step harder than it needs to be.
- **Sign errors when the constant term is negative.** If the equation is $4x - 9 = 3$, you add 9 (not subtract). The constant on the left is $-9$, so its inverse is $+9$.
- **Not applying the operation to the entire right side.** In $2x + 5 = 13$, subtracting 5 gives $2x = 8$, not $2x = 13 - 5$ computed incorrectly as $7$.
